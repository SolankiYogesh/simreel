How to use a camera in the iOS Simulator

The iOS Simulator doesn’t include a camera, so any screen that needs one is blank or throws an error. Here is what happens in your code and how to give the Simulator a camera to test with.

Why the iOS Simulator has no camera

On a physical iPhone, AVCaptureDevice returns the front and back cameras. In the Simulator there is no camera hardware to expose, so device discovery finds nothing:

let device = AVCaptureDevice.default(.builtInWideAngleCamera, for: .video, position: .back)
print(device as Any)   // nil in the iOS Simulator

Your capture session then has no input, your preview layer stays black, and flows such as barcode scanning, ID capture or profile photos can’t be exercised without a real device.

Your options

  • Test on a physical device. Accurate, but slow for every small camera change.
  • Abstract the camera behind a protocol and inject a fake in debug builds. Works, but adds code and doesn’t exercise the real AVFoundation path.
  • Give the Simulator a camera. SimReel does this at the system level, so your app runs unchanged and uses the same capture APIs it uses on a device.

Use a camera in the Simulator with SimReel

  1. Install SimReel with brew install --cask SolankiYogesh/tap/simreel, or download it and drag it to Applications. It needs macOS 14+ on Apple Silicon, with Xcode installed.
  2. Open SimReel. Booted simulators appear in the window and are enabled automatically.
  3. Run your app in the Simulator and open its camera screen. If the app was already running, relaunch it.
  4. In SimReel, choose a source: an image, a video, a QR code or your Mac camera. Your app receives it immediately.

The first time you use the Mac camera source, macOS asks for camera permission. The Simulator also shows its normal camera-permission prompt for your app — allow it, as you would on a device.

Check that your app sees a camera

let discovery = AVCaptureDevice.DiscoverySession(
    deviceTypes: [.builtInWideAngleCamera], mediaType: .video, position: .unspecified)
print(discovery.devices.map(\.localizedName))   // ["Back Camera", "Front Camera"]

let session = AVCaptureSession()
if let device = AVCaptureDevice.default(for: .video),
   let input = try? AVCaptureDeviceInput(device: device),
   session.canAddInput(input) {
    session.addInput(input)
}
let preview = AVCaptureVideoPreviewLayer(session: session)   // shows your chosen source

Choosing what the camera shows

  • Image — a PNG or JPEG, such as an ID card, a receipt or a product photo.
  • Video — an MP4 or MOV that loops, with rotation metadata respected.
  • QR code — any text or URL, rendered as a scannable code. See how to test QR scanning.
  • Mac camera — your built-in, external or Continuity Camera webcam.

Each source can be set for the front camera, the back camera, or both. Images and videos can be cropped to fill the frame or fit inside it with padding and a background colour.

What works

SimReel answers the capture APIs most apps use: AVCaptureSession, AVCaptureVideoPreviewLayer, AVCaptureVideoDataOutput (BGRA and NV12 frames), AVCaptureMetadataOutput (QR codes) and AVCapturePhotoOutput (JPEG). Video recording, depth data, Live Photos and ARKit are not supported. See the full list.

Troubleshooting

  • Still no camera? Check that SimReel shows Camera active for that simulator, then relaunch your app.
  • Grey frames? The SimReel app has quit. Open it again.
  • Using React Native, Expo or Flutter? They build on AVFoundation so they should work, but this hasn’t been verified yet — please report what you find.
